# The story behind ChillScreen

I like looking at calm places. Pastures, sunsets, mountain ranges, the night sky, the sea floor, the kind of picture that makes you think slowly. And when I work, I always have YouTube playing music in the background.

One day I asked for the obvious thing: an app I could open while working that would play my playlists and show those places on the screen. Better if the pictures moved, like a film, and maybe with a few small tools next to them, like notes and tasks.

## The first idea was a fish tank

Real scenery photos and videos are lovely, but they are static or they loop, and I get bored fast. What I wanted was something alive that I could glance at: a little ecosystem. So ChillScreen started as an aquarium, with fish, snails and shrimp that live their own lives, built to run quietly in the background.

It was not enough. I wanted the feeling of those Makoto Shinkai films, *Your Name* and *Weathering with You*, the light, the weather, the sky doing something. A fish tank is not that.

## A room with a window

The change that made it click was a simple one: stop trying to be a painting, and be a room. You sit in a room, and there is a window in front of you. Rain runs down the glass and you can wipe it with the mouse. The time of day shows in the middle, like the weather on a phone's home screen, and the weather reacts to it.

Then I asked for it to be pixel art, and then I asked again, because the text was too pixelated to read. Taste matters when you are designing an ambience, and every small thing counts: the music player is a little radio that sits on the window sill, not a web component stuck on top. The notes and tasks float over the scene with no boxes around them.

## Calm is the point

The work I do while ChillScreen is open on the other screen is already hard enough. So the rules are strict:

- Everything is small and simple, and the details hide until you need them.
- Nothing flashes, nags or jumps. Motion is slow, and wind moves the leaves and clouds.
- The mouse never shifts the picture around. It only makes the animals notice you.
- It runs in a browser tab, stays light enough to leave open all day, and remembers everything, so you never set it up twice.

There are two modes. **Zen** hides everything except the scene. **Business** brings back notes on the left and tasks, reminders and a focus timer around it.

## Then it grew

Once the window existed, I wanted to travel through it. So there are journeys that go on for a long time, each with its own cast and things to do: a road through six countries, a night train through the snow, a canal barge, a rainy night bus, a jungle river, an airship, a fairy road. And there are landscapes that stay still but have depth and life: a seaside, a pine forest, a mirror lake, cherry blossoms, a fishing spot, interstellar space, an alpine peak, a snowy cabin, a zen garden and a rooftop city.

A day-and-night control, like the one in Minecraft, lets you speed the sun up or slow it down.

## How it was made

I built ChillScreen in about two days, working with AI coding tools: Claude Code for planning, building and checking, and Codex for the larger builds, each on its own branch. I wrote what I wanted and what felt wrong, looked at screenshots, and sent things back until they felt right. The scenes are all drawn in code, with no image files.

## Why open source

Other people look at calm places while they work too. I would like ChillScreen to be theirs as well: change it, add a scene of your own, put it on a spare screen. If you make a place worth sitting in front of, send it back.
